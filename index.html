These are the “stylized facts”: statistical fingerprints shared by real stock, currency and crypto markets. Nobody coded them into Flash. They emerge from 500 traders and an order book, measured live from this session’s one-minute returns.

The exchange is a real matching engine: a limit order book with price-time priority, where every price level is a first-in-first-out queue. Every trade moves cash and shares between two accounts, and the totals are conserved to the share. A limit-up/limit-down band stops prints more than 8% from a slow reference price.

The traders follow the Chiarella–Iori model. Each of 500 agents mixes three motives with personal weights: value (pull toward a fundamental price that moves with bursts of news), trend (extrapolate recent returns, or bet against them) and noise. They form an expected price and either rest a patient order or take liquidity near the touch.

The market makers quote both sides around a blend of the last price and their estimate of fair value, lean against their inventory and never cross the market. That keeps calm trading efficient: the last minute says little about the next. When volatility spikes they pull everything and wait. That withdrawal, together with traders who arrive faster after big moves, is what turns a large sell order into a flash crash.

Built from scratch: matching engine, agents and statistics, with no libraries.
